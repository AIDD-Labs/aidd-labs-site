The SURE (Summer Undergraduate Research Experience) program brings together undergraduate researchers from across the University of Michigan College of Engineering to conduct faculty-mentored research over the summer, culminating in a showcase where students present their work to the broader university community.

Olivia presented her poster, "Leveraging Qualitative Methods for Community-Centered Design." Her work explores how qualitative coding and analysis methods can support community-centered design, using interview data from the AIDD and Edge Lab's ongoing Sarasota parametric insurance project as a working example. The core argument of the poster is that you can't design something that fits a community's real needs if you only ever look at data and statistics; you also have to listen to the users' experiences and concerns. Over the summer, Olivia developed a reflexivity statement, built a foundation in qualitative coding practices, helped draft an interview guide for local stakeholders and experts in Sarasota, and began coding interviews to pull out key patterns in how people talk about risk, trust, and financial recovery. Early findings show that qualitative coding helps illuminate the gap between what designers assume a community needs and what the community actually wants, a distinction that directly shapes the co-design approach of the Sarasota project going forward.
