### Abstract
After an earthquake, the United States Geological Survey (USGS) produces near-real-time information on earthquake impacts through the publicly available PAGER (Prompt Assessment of Global Earthquakes for Response) loss estimation system. While PAGER was primarily designed for a targeted group of users—emergency responders, government and aid agencies, and the media—in reality, it reaches a much larger audience with tens of thousands of online viewers. In this study, we sought to understand how accessible and useful PAGER is to users with different backgrounds than the initial targeted user groups. This research was motivated by a concern that additional potential user groups were not being reached nor were they receiving the information that would be most useful to them. We draw on 16 focus groups with 49 disaster risk reduction practitioners who work with socially vulnerable groups in addition to USGS web traffic, and email lists. Our analysis of web traffic and email lists reveals that PAGER is widely accessed by users outside of earthquake-affected areas and in sectors outside of the user groups for which PAGER was initially designed. Our analysis of the focus group data indicated that PAGER could be more accessible to additional key user groups by making the information relatable, transparent, understandable, and shareable. Importantly, we find there are limits to the usability of the currently available PAGER information, where community-based organizations found it difficult to see how PAGER information could be helpful given their extensive on-the-ground knowledge. Considering these results, we offer strategies for developing post-earthquake loss information that is more accessible to a wider range of users while respecting the limits of a rapid loss estimation system such as PAGER.
