### Abstract
Communities globally are struggling with flooding and water pollution due to climate change, driving them to experiment with new technologies and practices such as green infrastructure, a low-cost alternative to traditional steel and concrete stormwater infrastructure. While participation in urban stormwater management is expanding, access to the digital technologies needed to support this expansion remains limited to trained engineers. This study examines the use of digital stormwater modeling in Detroit, Michigan (USA), exploring the opportunities and challenges of making stormwater data and technologies more accessible to non-engineers, particularly those rooted in the communities they serve. We introduced a customized stormwater modeling interface and elicited feedback through a user study with 17 green infrastructure practitioners. Results indicate that the customized interface allows users to independently produce stormwater modeling data that leverages their local expertise, thus expanding participation in the otherwise siloed planning of climate adaptation and flood prevention.
