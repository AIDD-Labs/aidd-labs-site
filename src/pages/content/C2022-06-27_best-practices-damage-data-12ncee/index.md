### Abstract
Researchers often state that “perishable” data need to be collected after a disaster occurs, often referencing the collection of accurate damage information before recovery begins. Damage data, however, are perishable in a different way—they are collected, but they perish due to lack of use during an event. In this study, we review the multitude of damage data sources available—and their potential uses—at different time points after an earthquake occurs. We then ask, have these various data sources been applied to these use cases after past events? Here, we focus on the acquisition and use of damage data after the 2021 Haiti earthquake as compared to previous events, including the 2010 Haiti earthquake. As a community of damage information providers, we find that we are moving in the right direction so that the damage data we acquire can simultaneously serve multiple purposes, ranging from scientific understanding to post-event response and recovery planning. From these events and personal experiences, we close with a set of recommendations and best practices for future post-disaster data collection.
