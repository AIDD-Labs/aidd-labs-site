### Abstract
As natural hazards increase in frequency and severity, traditional indemnity-based insurers are retreating from high-risk areas, leaving many homeowners without adequate financial protection. Alternative models, such as parametric reciprocal insurance, offer potential solutions to disaster financing gaps yet remain largely underutilized at the household level in the United States (U.S.). This study investigates the feasibility of implementing a community-based parametric reciprocal insurance program. Through engagement with households and key stakeholders, including business owners and local government, we examine current post-disaster financing challenges and identify design needs for a novel insurance model in Sarasota. If successful, this approach could expand financial protection in high-risk areas by enabling rapid, predefined payouts and covering a wider array of disaster-related losses beyond physical damage, offering timely and effective relief to those most in need.
